## Result and recommendation (hand-written; the tables below are generated)

**The question.** Playtest v0.1.5: skill ranges do not fit a 7x7 Board (range 4 reaches almost every tile), so there is no approach phase. Three ways were measured alone and combined: (a) shorter ranges in data, (b) disadvantage on attack rolls from distance 4, (c) mountains block line of sight (plus four more mountain tiles per Board). 64,000 Battles: 8 variants x 4 sets x 2,000, both sides the in-game AI. The variants exist only inside the simulator; data/ and the rules of the game are unchanged (`ENGINE_VERSION` 0.10.0).

**What the numbers say**

1. **Today's balance is already inside the targets** (team A 43.2-57.1%, turn limit at most 12.6%, in all four sets). Balance is therefore not what separates the variants; it only rules some out.
2. **No variant creates an approach phase on 7x7.** The new metric (Turns over before the first attack roll, mean over Battles) is 0.08 today and at most 0.16 with any variant. On Shallows it is 0.00 under every variant: the first attack comes in the very first Turn, because Move plus range still covers the distance between the start zones. Shortening ranges (a) or adding disadvantage (b) moves it by +0.01 at most; they trade accuracy for nothing the metric can see.
3. **Only (c) moves it at all**, and only on Crossroads: S2 Crossroads 0.00 -> 0.21, S1 Crossroads 0.32 -> 0.37 (b+c: 0.42, a+b+c: 0.44). The four extra mountains on Shallows are not on the lines between the start zones, so Shallows does not change.
4. **(a) is the worst option.** Shorter ranges alone put S1 Crossroads at 19.6% turn limit (target: 15% at most) and a+c pushes team A to 62.5-65.1% in two sets (target: 40-60%). Shorter ranges also make Burst, Counter and Hit and Run more common (Burst 1.91 -> 2.29 per Battle), i.e. fights get closer, which is the opposite of what the playtest asked for.
5. **(b) is balance-neutral but does not help the goal.** It lowers team A by 1 to 4 pp in every set (S1 Shallows 43.2% -> 40.6%, at the edge of the target) and leaves the first-attack Turn where it was. Its cost: a new source in the roll-mode preview that the UI must explain.

**Recommendation: (c) alone, without (a) and (b).** Mountains that block line of sight are the one change that makes positioning matter on Crossroads without hurting balance (4 / 4 sets, A 48.9-56.0%, turn limit at most 14.5%). It is also the smallest set of changes: one engine rule (a tile of a given terrain blocks sight, the engine switch already exists), the extra mountain tiles as data on the Boards, and no new roll-mode source for the UI to explain. b+c is marginally better balanced (the limit drops to 11.8%) but adds a second rule for no gain on the goal; keep it as the fallback if c alone plays too long in the playtest.

**Be honest about the size of the effect.** On Crossroads c moves the first attack out of the first Turn in about one more Battle in five in Scenario 2 (0.00 -> 0.21) and about one more in twenty in Scenario 1 (0.32 -> 0.37); on Shallows it does nothing. The real lever for "the Board is too small for these ranges" is a bigger Board (tickets 14-16: 9x9). If the user wants a stronger effect on 7x7, the next experiment is more or better placed sight blockers on Shallows (and a few more on Crossroads), not shorter ranges.

**What choosing (c) means for ticket 09.** Rule change: terrain tiles of mountain block line of sight (rules §7, new D-row, `ENGINE_VERSION` minor bump, the engine switch becomes the rule). Data change: the four extra mountain tiles per Board (`EXTRA_MOUNTAINS` in tools/simulator/src/variants.ts, as measured), decision log row with these results; Wispling and other Flying attackers still see over mountains (as over Units). A Unit standing on a mountain is not blocked by its own tile. UI: Codex shows blocked shots with the existing "blocked" display (the rejection is `no-line-of-sight` with no blocking Unit). The visual fixtures need the usual refresh after the bump.

**Caveats.** The extra mountains also change terrain effects (mountain is an element: terrain match gives Wispling advantage, others disadvantage against it), so c measures sight blocking and those four tiles together. The first-attack metric is a mean over Battles, so it cannot show how the Battles are distributed; most are at 0. The AI was not changed for sight-blocking terrain (it reads legal actions and previews from the engine), so it does not yet plan to use cover.

Command (reproduces the tables): `corepack pnpm sim ranges --games 2000 --seed 0`, about 17 minutes on 16 threads.
